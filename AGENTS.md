# Handover: "Don't Queue At The Bar"

A comedy campaign site against single-file queueing at the bar in UK pubs, bars and breweries.
Hosted on Cloudflare Workers, on the team's current Cloudflare account (set up by a co-worker). The first deploy was on a previous personal account at `dont-queue-at-the-bar.cowen19921.workers.dev`, which is no longer used. Add the live URL here once confirmed.

## The idea (read this first)

- **Background:** Since COVID (2020 social-distancing stickers, single-file lines), people queue in one long line to the bar. Pre-COVID, you spread out along the bar and the barperson or landlord knows who is next.
- **Problem:** The single file wastes room in the pub, blocks seating, leaves most of the bar and staff idle, and is unnatural.
- **Goal:** Get people to stop, in a funny way. The site is a petition plus a merch route (beer mats for pubs).
- **Name:** "Don't Queue At The Bar". It was earlier called "Stop Queueing at the Bar" and "Bar the Line / The Bar Party" (see the old notes at the bottom).
- **Name for the right behaviour:** **The Cluster**. The wrong behaviour is **The Single File**.
- **Tone:** Direct, dry British humour. Short, punchy lines.
- **Gender-neutral language (always):** Never use gendered terms for pub staff or the people in the jokes. Say **barperson** (never barman, barmaid, bartender-as-"he"), **bar staff**, or **landlord** (neutral here). Refer to staff with **they/them/their**, never he/she/him/his/her. This applies to all copy: page text, meta/OG/Twitter tags, `site.webmanifest`, beer mats (`scripts/build-mats.mjs`), the petition and any new docs. Before finishing any copy change, grep `public/`, `scripts/` and `src/` for `barman|barmen|barmaid|\bhe\b|\bhis\b|\bhim\b|\bshe\b|\bher\b`.
- **Look:** Very bold. Huge lowercase type. One idea per full-screen colour block, scrolling down a long page. Simple diagrams with animation. No photos.
- **Brand marks (settled 12 Oct 2026, see `brand/BRIEF.md` for the rules):** the primary mark is **the Cluster roundel** (three people seen from behind, spread along a bar that overhangs a ring; one colour, no words). Its partner is **the type stamp** ("don’t queue / at the bar." in a ring). On the site the marks are ink or cream only, flat, never a tint, outline or shadow. The roundel is the favicon, the app icons, the Stripe icon and the hazard licence seal; the horizontal lockup (roundel plus name) sits top left of the homepage; the stamp leads on the mat front and flyers. The drawing lives once, in `scripts/lib/brand.mjs`, and three scripts write from it: `node brand/final/build.mjs` (the brand folder, black on white plus the reversed set), `npm run build:icons` (`public/mark.svg`, `public/lockup-horizontal.svg`, the icons) and `npm run build:mats` (`public/mats/`). Don't redraw the mark by hand: edit the module and re-run all three. Clear space is an eighth of the mark's width; minimum sizes are 16px for the mark, 40px tall for the horizontal lockup and 48px for the stamp.

## Facts and claims

The maths and history screens quote real, dated sources, each with a visible linked source line on the page and in the footer's sources line. **`RESEARCH.md` is the register:** every figure, date and quote on the site has a row there with its source, type, confidence and a click-through box, plus the derived arithmetic, reserve figures and an avoid list. Nothing factual goes on the site without a row in it. Every row is still unverified (the research could only read search extracts), so work through its before-promoting checklist before the site is promoted.

- The animated sim scores and the maths screen are illustrative, and the page says so ("worked example", "Illustrative, like the sims").
- The laws quoted are England's; the page says so and notes that Wales was the last to drop table service.
- COVID normalised the bar queue; it didn't invent it, and the rules never said to queue at the bar. Keep the copy that way.
- **Hackney Church Brew Co is deliberately unused** (its +25% and "three times as long" are the brewery's own claims, and other outlets reported different numbers). The East London Times article earlier docs cited could not be found; don't cite it.

To re-test, render `public/mats/mat-back.svg` to PNG (headless Chromium) and decode it with jsQR or zxing-cpp, installed outside the repo. Before printing, convert text to outlines on a Mac, add the printer's bleed, and scan a printed proof of the back.

## How the interactive bits work (`public/script.js`)

- **Reveal:** `.reveal` sections fade up via IntersectionObserver. The `js` class on `<html>` gates this, so the page works without JS.
- **Pub scenes:** `.scene[data-mode="single"|"cluster"]` run a small simulation (seeded random, same arrivals, 4 staff). In single mode only one member of staff can reach the queue front, so it serves far fewer pints. Each served customer gets a pint slid across the bar, then walks off with it. Idle staff sway side to side; in the cluster, busy staff slide along the bar to their customer and some customers stand a row back (`BACK_ROW_CHANCE`) until a gap opens. It starts and stops with visibility. Tunables are the constants near `STAFF_X`, `SERVICE_TICKS`, `ARRIVAL_NONE`/`ARRIVAL_PAIR`, `BACK_ROW_CHANCE`.
- **How to stage (`#howto`):** the section is 4.5 viewports tall and `.howto-stick` (a `.screen`, `position: sticky`) stays on screen while it scrolls past. `script.js` cuts the scroll through the section into five equal slices (intro, then steps 1 to 4) and sets on the stage: the cumulative classes `s1`..`s4`, the block colour (`c-cream`, `c-blue` for steps 1 to 3, `c-green` for step 4; no amber block, so the amber ring, sightline and pint stay the only amber), `--k` (0 to 1 through the current slice, used for the scroll-linked walk in step 1, the sightline draw in step 3 and the pint slide in step 4), `--sp` (the fill of the track through the step pills) and `served` once the pint reaches you (`SERVED_AT`). The copy for the intro and each step is stacked in one grid cell and shown with `.active`; the pills are plain anchor links to `#howto-N`, empty `.howto-at` markers that `styles.css` places at the start of each step's slice, so the browser does the scrolling and no script moves the page. Everything in the pub diagram (`.howto-pub`, reusing `.dot`, `.scene-bar`, `.pint`, `.has-drink`) moves by CSS transitions off those classes, so it reverses when scrolling back up. The markup ships the finished frame (`s1 s2 s3 s4 served`, green) and all four steps stacked, which is what visitors without JS see; `beforeprint` prints the same. The only self-running animation is the gap ring's pulse, stopped under reduced motion. The outer section must never get `overflow: hidden` or the sticky stops working.
- **Timeline rail:** computed from scroll position. It hides on the hero, fills dot to dot, marks the active chapter (`.active`) and passed ones (`.done`).
- **Bars (`#maths`):** the fills grow from 0 when the screen is revealed (CSS `clip-path` on `.js .reveal .fill`), the single file first. Full width with reduced motion or no JS. On ink the cluster bar is amber, not green (Snug Green on ink is too low contrast).
- **Days since (`[data-since]`, the today stop):** at load the script writes the real figure ("1,911 days" on 12 Oct 2026, counted by local calendar day from 19 Jul 2021) into `.count-txt`, so screen readers, print and copy always get it. The count-up is visual only: an `aria-hidden` twin (`.count-vis`, shown through `data-shown`) runs while `.is-counting` hides the real text visually, then clears. Without JS the HTML says "over five years".
- **History strip (`.strip`):** a native horizontal scroller with `scroll-snap`, so it swipes and scrolls with no JS. The script adds the prev/next buttons (`aria-disabled` at the ends, never `disabled`, so focus stays), ArrowLeft/ArrowRight/Home/End when the strip itself has focus, the "n of 8" counter (`aria-live`) and the `.current` sticker. The strip starts at the content gutter and bleeds to the right only, with `scroll-padding-right: var(--sx)`, so the rail never sits on it. The rail's active label is hidden on `#maths` and `#history` unless the rail is hovered or focused. Stickers are slapped down once on reveal (about a second, so no pause control needed). `@media print` wraps all eight stops.
- **Petition form:** POSTs JSON to `/api/sign`, and shows the running total from `/api/count`.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected throughout: it stops the sims (they show a still, pre-rolled frame), the scroll hint, the promo sticker pulse, the mat wobble and the reveals. There is no on-page pause button; it was removed on request, so visitors without reduced motion can't stop the looping animations (a WCAG 2.2.2 gap).
- **No autoscroll:** nothing moves the page on its own (no scroll snap, no `scrollTo`/`scrollIntoView`, no wheel or key handlers). Only anchor links the visitor clicks scroll smoothly (`scroll-behavior: smooth`). The one exception is sideways and inside a component: the history strip has its own horizontal `scroll-snap`, and its buttons and arrow keys scroll only the strip, only when pressed.
- **Poll:** `GET /api/poll` on load: a browser that has voted gets `{ voted, choice, results }` and goes straight to the results; anyone else gets `{ voted: false }` and no numbers. Pressing a button POSTs `{ choice: 'cluster' | 'single' }` (`cluster` is For, stop the single file; `single` is Against, keep it). The stored values name the side, not the button, so rewording the question never touches the data and the answer carries the results. A vote that isn't counted still sees the tally: `{ voted: false, capped: true, ... }` over a daily cap, or `{ voted: false, missing: true, ... }` for a change (`{ choice, change: true }`) with no vote on record, so a change can never become a second vote. After a vote the kicker goes and the question steps down to `.big` (`#poll.voted`) so the results fit a laptop screen: a lead line chosen from the raw counts ("Early days" under 50 votes, then who's ahead, or a dead heat), the bar chart, one quiet line with the total and the "self-selecting and unscientific" caveat, a quip and one next step (Sign the petition for For, Take the test for Against). "Changed your mind?" brings the buttons back (the question stays small, the side on record gets the "your vote" stamp, focus goes to the note) with "Keep my vote" to go back; the next vote replaces the old one. The lead lines and quips are in `script.js` and again in `pollWords` in `src/worker.js` for the no-JS page: keep them in step. Without JS the buttons post the form and the Worker answers with a small results page with the same chart. Results keep the colour meanings (green is For, the Cluster; red is Against, the Single File); the two buttons are equal ink pills so the question stays fair.
- **Tell a mate:** after a successful signature the form shows a share button (native share sheet, or copies the link).
- **Security headers:** the CSP, `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy` and `Permissions-Policy` live in one place, the `/*` block of `public/_headers`. Cloudflare applies it to everything served from `public/` (pages, CSS, JS, audio, images, 404s), including the home page the Worker fetches through `env.ASSETS` and rewrites, because HTMLRewriter keeps the headers. Cloudflare reads the file and never serves it. `_headers` rules never reach responses the Worker builds itself, so `src/worker.js` imports the file as text (the `rules` entry in `wrangler.jsonc`) and copies the `/*` block onto its `/api` JSON. The Worker won't start, so a deploy fails, if that block loses its CSP. If you add an external script, stylesheet, font, image, audio file, iframe or analytics beacon, add its origin to the matching directive in `public/_headers` (`script-src`, `style-src`, `font-src`, `img-src`, `media-src`, `frame-src`, `connect-src`) or the browser will block it. Nothing else needs changing. Inline `<script>` and `on*` attributes are blocked. Inline `style` attributes are allowed. Plain outbound links need no change. After a deploy, check with `curl -sI <site>/hazard`.
- Design and product context live in `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md` and `.impeccable/design.json`.

## Hazard perception tests (`hazard*.html`)

- **Pages:** `hazard.html` is the hub, linked from the hero promo sticker and the home page `#test` section. Each test page loads `styles.css`, `hazard.css` and `hazard-kit.css` (the barperson test also its own one-rule CSS for the wall colour), then `hazard.js`, `hazard-kit.js` and its own JS (all `defer`). The hub doors are centred posters with `<h2 class="big">` headings. The result screen covers the stage with solid Scene Night. **Brand:** every hazard page (hub, both tests, the clip preview) starts its header (`.hz-top`) with the cream lockup (`a.hz-brand`, `lockup-horizontal-reversed.svg`, 40px tall, linking home, accessible name "Don’t Queue At The Bar, home"). On the hub it is the only thing in the header; on the tests the "← All tests" pill sits beside it and the test's name goes right, hidden at 760px and below where the three won't fit. Short landscape phones (44px header) crop it to the roundel at 32px with `object-fit`. Each page also carries the homepage's icon and manifest tags.
- **Layout (`hazard.css`):** in landscape (desktop and landscape phones) the clip and playhead sit on the left and the questions on the right. In portrait the clip is at the top (sticky) and the questions sit underneath. The stage is sized to fit the viewport height in landscape. Short landscape phones get the compact overrides at the end of the file.
- **Clips:** each `.hpt-shot` in a test page is one clip, made of CSS keyframes. Everything in a shot inherits the clip's duration (`--dur`), and `hazard.js` plays, pauses and seeks them through the Web Animations API (`getAnimations`, `currentTime`), so each clip stops exactly on its hazard. Each shot needs an `<i class="hpt-clock">`.
- **Data:** `HazardTest.init(root, { clips, pick, grades, links, posterAt, ambience })`. `clips` is the pool (10 per test). Each run deals `pick` of them (default 5) in random order, and every retake deals again. A clip is `{ dur, cue, label, sounds: [[ms, name]], question, options: [{ text, ok, say }] }`. `dur` and `cue` default to 10s and 5s. There are no on-screen captions, so a clip tells its story with movement and speech bubbles. `label` is the stage's screen reader description. The outcome after the cue always shows the right behaviour and ends on a settled frame. Options are not shuffled, so vary which letter is right. Grades are fractions of the dealt clips. `ambience` is an optional sound name looped under the whole test (both tests use `'pub'`; see Audio). Keep the `.hpt-shot` elements in the same order and number as `clips`.
- **Reviewing a clip:** add `?clips=3,7` (pool positions, counting from 1) to a test URL to play just those clips, in that order.
- **Player chrome:** `hazard.js` adds the start cover, hazard badge and controls (play/pause, progress bar with a diamond per hazard, time, mute) itself.
- **Audio (all from ElevenLabs, no browser speech or synthesised tones):** the player plays narration and sound effects only from `public/audio/manifest.json`. A line or sound that's missing stays silent and logs a `console.warn`. `HazardTest.spokenLines()` and `soundNames()` list everything a test can play, and the scripts use the same functions. The voice and its settings are in `scripts/voice.json` (currently "Publican Yorkshire"), and the sound effect prompts are in `scripts/sfx.json`. **Ambience:** the config's `ambience` sound (the `pub` entry in `sfx.json`, a 20s murmur recorded with ElevenLabs' `loop: true`) loops at gain 0.3 (`AMBIENCE_GAIN` in `hazard.js`) under narration and effects at full level. It fades in when the test starts and keeps going through questions, outcomes and the hold before Next. It stops when the viewer pauses or mutes, when the player is away (scrolled out of view or tab hidden, even mid-question), and on the result screen, so the fanfare plays clean. It comes back on play, on return, on unmute and on Next. How each line is performed lives in `scripts/directions.json`: ElevenLabs audio tags, pauses and emphasis, keyed by the exact on-screen text, with a default tag per role (question, correct, wrong, result by score, narration) for lines without their own entry. Directions are only sent on `eleven_v3`/`eleven_v4` (v2 would read the tags aloud), and they must not change the words. `npm run audio:compare` renders a small before/after set into `.voice-samples/directions/`. After changing any copy, prompt or voice, run `npm run audio:generate`. It reads `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` from `.env`, only re-records what changed, and fails if anything is left without audio. Commit the new files in `public/audio/`. `audio:dry` shows what would be recorded and the character count. `audio:check` checks for gaps. `audio:samples` renders candidate voices into `.voice-samples/`, which is gitignored. Mute is saved in `localStorage`.
- **Illustration kit (`hazard-kit.js`, `hazard-kit.css`), the "Public Information Cartoon":** both tests draw their clips from one kit, loaded after `hazard.js` and before the test's own script. The test script builds the clips first (`HazardKit.build(root, clips)`, which needs each clip's `dur` and `cue`), then calls `HazardTest.init`. Rules and palette roles are in DESIGN.md under "Hazard test illustration". Every clip in both pools is drawn by the kit; the old top-down dots and `bm-*` scenery are gone.
  - **Cast:** `CAST` in the kit, ids `c1`–`c12` (customers) and `s1`–`s4` (staff), each with a fixed skin, hair, top, build and height. In the customer test "you" is always `c12`. Neutral ids, no gender. Tops are never ink or bar wood (they vanish on the floor and counter).
  - **Set and cameras:** `<div class="hk-set" data-set="room">` is the room seen from behind the bar (staff-side camera, `data-cam="sc"`: back wall, door, WC door, lamps, pool table, a table, the counter nearest you). `data-set="bar"` is the back bar seen from the room (customer-side camera, `data-cam="cc"`: shelves, mirror, taps, till, the counter). The room's upper wall carries flat decor (frames, a chalkboard, a dartboard, lamps) so the space above the heads isn't dead; the floor line is at 44% and the counter at 76%, which keeps people at the counter filling the middle of the frame. Put `data-cam` on the shot (or on a `.hk-cut` block). Lanes give depth: `sc` lanes 0 (at the counter) to 3 (back wall) plus `f` (a colleague on your side of the counter, seen from behind, feet below the frame); `cc` lanes 0 (at the counter, seen from behind), 1 to 3 (each nearer the camera: a queue running toward the camera) and `s` (staff behind the counter). `LANES` maps a lane to feet height, scale and z-index. The wall colour is `--wall` on `.hpt-stage` (pink in the customer test, blue in the barperson test, set in each test's CSS).
  - **Figures:** `<i class="hk-person" data-cast="c7" data-view="front|back" data-pos="70,0" data-act="...">`. `data-pos` is `x,lane` where the figure ends up. `data-you` adds the cream YOU pill. A bubble for that speaker goes inside the element: `<p class="hk-bub" data-side="l|r" data-on="2300-5500">…</p>`. Your own lines in the barperson test are `<p class="hk-bub me" style="--x:30" data-on="5300-6700">` as direct children of the shot.
  - **Acting (`data-act`):** `"t: token token; t: token"` in ms. Each cast member has a stride period from `HazardKit.pace(cast)` (380–560 ms: taller and broader regulars longer, grey-haired ones slower), a stride covers 0.6 of the figure's height (0.4 when carrying), and the walk is always a whole number of strides, so a walker ends with both feet down. `go(x,lane,ms)` makes the time an override (still rounded to whole strides); `pace(ms)` before a `go` sets a hurried or lazy pace for that figure from then on. The gait is picked from the direction: a side-on waddle (body rock, hop, leg lifts), a towards/away walk (shoulder sway, lift, the depth scale changing each step) or a careful carrying walk (arms locked on the props, short steps, less bob). The figure travels while a foot is up and stops as it lands, faces the way it is going during a sideways walk, counter-swings its arms (not when carrying), squashes on landing, and the head and any bun lag the body a little. With the legs hidden (behind the counter) the shoulder rock, head and arms still say "walking". ## Backend

- **Database:** Neon Postgres (eu-west-2), table `signatures` (`id`, `name`, `email`, `consent`, `consent_at`, `created_at`). The unique index on `lower(email)` means duplicate emails are silently ignored, so the API doesn't reveal who has signed.
- **Hyperdrive:** id `88c5a2e2902f470995344cd16411c3fc` in the current Cloudflare account (the earlier account had `bar-petition`, id `7918ba51…`, which is no longer used). Use Neon's **direct** (non-`-pooler`) host. Hyperdrive does the pooling. It is bound as `HYPERDRIVE` in `wrangler.jsonc`.
- **Worker:** `src/worker.js` checks the Origin header, has a honeypot field `website`, validates name, email and consent, and caches `/api/count` for 60s. `/api/*` and `/` run the Worker first (`run_worker_first`), and so does any path with no matching file (it returns the 404 from `env.ASSETS`). Everything else is served straight from `public/`.
- **Poll (`/api/poll`):** `poll_votes` holds `choice`, a random `voter` UUID (unique) and `created_at`. That is pseudonymous (the id sits in the voter's cookie), not anonymous, so say so in the privacy notice. Protection against stuffing: the `POLL_LIMITER` rate-limit binding in `wrangler.jsonc` (10 votes a minute per network, before the database); one vote per browser through the `dq_poll` cookie (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, scoped to `/api/poll`, a year), where voting again changes the vote (and rotates the voter id) and never adds one or uses the allowance; and daily caps counted in `poll_limits`: 20 per network (`POLL_VOTES_PER_NETWORK_PER_DAY`; an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64, since one home or phone can hop around a whole /64) and 200 per IPv6 /48 (`POLL_VOTES_PER_IPV6_48_PER_DAY`; one person can hold a /48 and its 65,536 /64s). The caps are generous because a pub's wifi or a phone network can put many people behind one address; a vote over them isn't counted, still sees the tally, and logs "Poll vote over the daily cap" (count those in the Worker logs to see whether real people hit it). The Origin check only stops other websites posting votes for their visitors; scripts can still post, and Turnstile is the next step if they do. `poll_limits` never stores the address: its key is a SHA-256 of `POLL_SECRET`, the UTC day and the network. A daily Cron Trigger (00:05 UTC, `scheduled()` in `src/worker.js`) deletes past days. Without `POLL_SECRET` the Worker refuses votes (503, "The poll isn't open yet") rather than store hashes anyone could reverse; for `wrangler dev`, put `POLL_SECRET=anything` in `.dev.vars` (gitignored). Hyperdrive caches read queries for up to a minute, so the totals query is keyed on the voter (a cached answer always includes their own vote, and a changed vote gets a new id), and a new or changed vote gets its totals from the INSERT or UPDATE itself.
- **Secrets:** the connection string is stored in Hyperdrive only. It is not in the repo. Never commit it.

## Gotchas

- **Hyperdrive error 10157 (config not found):** the id in `wrangler.jsonc` must exist in the account Wrangler is logged into. If `wrangler deploy` fails with 10157, you are probably logged into the wrong account (`npx wrangler whoami`), or the id changed. Check with `npx wrangler hyperdrive list`. `wrangler dev` may rewrite the id.
- **Old account leftovers:** the previous personal account may still host `dont-queue-at-the-bar` and the older `stop-queueing-at-the-bar` Workers, plus the `bar-petition` Hyperdrive config. They are unused. Clean them up there if you still have access.
- **Testing animations:** In an automated or hidden browser tab, scroll events and IntersectionObservers don't fire reliably. Force them by dispatching a `scroll` event or adding `in` to `.reveal` elements.
- **History strip layout traps:** `.history` needs `grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1fr)` and the strip needs `min-width: 0`, or the strip's `max-content` width widens the screen's grid column and the header slides off-screen. Draw nothing inside the strip with `100vw` offsets (the floor tape is one `::before` on `.stops`): a `right: -100vw` pseudo-element adds hundreds of pixels of empty scroll past the last card. A Playwright element screenshot of a section taller than the viewport resizes the viewport and makes Chrome re-snap the strip, so capture strip states with a tall viewport.
- **`--sx`:** `.screen` side padding is the `--sx` custom property (6vw, 52px below 900px), which the strip uses to bleed into the right gutter. Change the padding there, not with a new `padding` rule.
- **Count endpoint:** the first call after a deploy once returned a Cloudflare 1042 error, then worked. Watch for it.
