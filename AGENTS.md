# Handover: "Don't Queue At The Bar"

A comedy campaign site against single-file queueing at the bar in UK pubs, bars and breweries.
Hosted on Cloudflare Workers, on the team's current Cloudflare account (set up by a co-worker). The first deploy was on a previous personal account at `dont-queue-at-the-bar.cowen19921.workers.dev`, which is no longer used. Add the live URL here once confirmed.

## The idea (read this first)

- **Background:** Since COVID (2020 social-distancing stickers, single-file lines), people queue in one long line to the bar. Pre-COVID, you spread out along the bar and the barperson or landlord knows who is next.
- **Problem:** The single file wastes room in the pub, blocks seating, leaves most of the bar and staff idle, and is unnatural.
- **Goal:** Get people to stop, in a funny way. The site is a petition plus a merch route (beer mats for pubs).
- **Name:** "Don't Queue At The Bar". It was earlier called "Stop Queueing at the Bar" and "Bar the Line / The Bar Party" (see the old notes at the bottom).
- **Name for the right behaviour:** **The Cluster**. The wrong behaviour is **The Single File**.
- **Tone:** Direct, dry British humour. Short, punchy lines. The format is mock-scientific (the "Bar Rule", rulings, alignment chart), copied from cuberule.com.
- **Gender-neutral language (always):** Never use gendered terms for pub staff or the people in the jokes. Say **barperson** (never barman, barmaid, bartender-as-"he"), **bar staff**, or **landlord** (neutral here). Refer to staff with **they/them/their**, never he/she/him/his/her. This applies to all copy: page text, meta/OG/Twitter tags, `site.webmanifest`, beer mats (`scripts/build-mats.mjs`), the petition and any new docs. Before finishing any copy change, grep `public/`, `scripts/` and `src/` for `barman|barmen|barmaid|\bhe\b|\bhis\b|\bhim\b|\bshe\b|\bher\b`.
- **Look:** Very bold. Huge lowercase type. One idea per full-screen colour block, scrolling down a long page. Simple diagrams with animation. No photos.

## Real facts the page uses

Source: [East London Times, 29 Aug 2026](https://eastlondontimes.co.uk/local/hackney/hackney-church-brew-co-sales-spike-after-single-file-queue-ban-hackney-2026/), which reports Hamish Glenn (Broadsheet London), MyLondon and YouGov.

- Hackney Church Brew Co posted on 14 Aug 2026: "A BAR IS NOT A POST OFFICE". Their bar is about 20 ft with 4 staff.
- Like-for-like sales rose **25%** on the following Thursday (£3.50 pints) when people spread along the bar. Footfall was steady; spend per head rose.
- YouGov: **40%** prefer single-file, **39%** prefer along the bar. London: only 37% back single-file. Midlands, North and Wales: 42%. Ages 25 to 49 favour along the bar most.
- The site is **not affiliated** with Hackney Church Brew Co and says so in the footer. Consider telling them.
- Everything else (the animated sim scores, alignment chart, rulings) is jokes.

## How the interactive bits work (`public/script.js`)

- **Reveal:** `.reveal` sections fade up via IntersectionObserver. The `js` class on `<html>` gates this, so the page works without JS.
- **Count-ups:** elements with `data-count` (plus optional `data-prefix` and `data-suffix`) animate when scrolled to.
- **Pub scenes:** `.scene[data-mode="single"|"cluster"]` run a small simulation (seeded random, same arrivals, 4 staff). In single mode only one member of staff can reach the queue front, so it serves far fewer pints. It starts and stops with visibility. Tunables are the constants near `STAFF_X`, `SERVICE_TICKS`, `ARRIVAL_CHANCE`.
- **Timeline rail:** computed from scroll position. It hides on the hero, fills dot to dot, marks the active chapter (`.active`) and passed ones (`.done`).
- **Petition form:** POSTs JSON to `/api/sign`, and shows the running total from `/api/count`.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected throughout. Otherwise a fixed **pause animations** button (built in `script.js`, hidden for reduced-motion users) stops the sims, scroll hint and mat wobble (WCAG 2.2.2).
- **Tell a mate:** after a successful signature the form shows a share button (native share sheet, or copies the link).
- **Security headers:** the CSP, `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy` and `Permissions-Policy` live in one place, the `/*` block of `public/_headers`. Cloudflare applies it to everything served from `public/` (pages, CSS, JS, audio, images, 404s), including the home page the Worker fetches through `env.ASSETS` and rewrites, because HTMLRewriter keeps the headers. Cloudflare reads the file and never serves it. `_headers` rules never reach responses the Worker builds itself, so `src/worker.js` imports the file as text (the `rules` entry in `wrangler.jsonc`) and copies the `/*` block onto its `/api` JSON. The Worker won't start, so a deploy fails, if that block loses its CSP. If you add an external script, stylesheet, font, image, audio file, iframe or analytics beacon, add its origin to the matching directive in `public/_headers` (`script-src`, `style-src`, `font-src`, `img-src`, `media-src`, `frame-src`, `connect-src`) or the browser will block it. Nothing else needs changing. Inline `<script>` and `on*` attributes are blocked. Inline `style` attributes are allowed. Plain outbound links need no change. After a deploy, check with `curl -sI <site>/hazard`.
- Design and product context live in `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md` and `.impeccable/design.json`.

## Hazard perception tests (`hazard*.html`)

- **Pages:** `hazard.html` is the hub, linked from the home page `#test` section. Each test page loads `styles.css`, `hazard.css` and `hazard-kit.css` (the barperson test also its own one-rule CSS for the wall colour), then `hazard.js`, `hazard-kit.js` and its own JS (all `defer`).
- **Layout (`hazard.css`):** in landscape (desktop and landscape phones) the clip and playhead sit on the left and the questions on the right. In portrait the clip is at the top (sticky) and the questions sit underneath. The stage is sized to fit the viewport height in landscape. Short landscape phones get the compact overrides at the end of the file.
- **Clips:** each `.hpt-shot` in a test page is one clip, made of CSS keyframes. Everything in a shot inherits the clip's duration (`--dur`), and `hazard.js` plays, pauses and seeks them through the Web Animations API (`getAnimations`, `currentTime`), so each clip stops exactly on its hazard. Each shot needs an `<i class="hpt-clock">`.
- **Data:** `HazardTest.init(root, { clips, pick, grades, links, posterAt, ambience })`. `clips` is the pool (10 per test). Each run deals `pick` of them (default 5) in random order, and every retake deals again. A clip is `{ dur, cue, label, sounds: [[ms, name]], question, options: [{ text, ok, say }] }`. `dur` and `cue` default to 10s and 5s. There are no on-screen captions, so a clip tells its story with movement and speech bubbles. `label` is the stage's screen reader description. The outcome after the cue always shows the right behaviour and ends on a settled frame. Options are not shuffled, so vary which letter is right. Grades are fractions of the dealt clips. `ambience` is an optional sound name looped under the whole test (both tests use `'pub'`; see Audio). Keep the `.hpt-shot` elements in the same order and number as `clips`.
- **Reviewing a clip:** add `?clips=3,7` (pool positions, counting from 1) to a test URL to play just those clips, in that order.
- **Player chrome:** `hazard.js` adds the start cover, hazard badge and controls (play/pause, progress bar with a diamond per hazard, time, mute) itself.
- **Audio (all from ElevenLabs, no browser speech or synthesised tones):** the player plays narration and sound effects only from `public/audio/manifest.json`. A line or sound that's missing stays silent and logs a `console.warn`. `HazardTest.spokenLines()` and `soundNames()` list everything a test can play, and the scripts use the same functions. The voice and its settings are in `scripts/voice.json` (currently "Publican Yorkshire"), and the sound effect prompts are in `scripts/sfx.json`. **Ambience:** the config's `ambience` sound (the `pub` entry in `sfx.json`, a 20s murmur recorded with ElevenLabs' `loop: true`) loops at gain 0.3 (`AMBIENCE_GAIN` in `hazard.js`) under narration and effects at full level. It fades in when the test starts and keeps going through questions, outcomes and the hold before Next. It stops when the viewer pauses or mutes, when the player is away (scrolled out of view or tab hidden, even mid-question), and on the result screen, so the fanfare plays clean. It comes back on play, on return, on unmute and on Next. How each line is performed lives in `scripts/directions.json`: ElevenLabs audio tags, pauses and emphasis, keyed by the exact on-screen text, with a default tag per role (question, correct, wrong, result by score, narration) for lines without their own entry. Directions are only sent on `eleven_v3`/`eleven_v4` (v2 would read the tags aloud), and they must not change the words. `npm run audio:compare` renders a small before/after set into `.voice-samples/directions/`. After changing any copy, prompt or voice, run `npm run audio:generate`. It reads `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` from `.env`, only re-records what changed, and fails if anything is left without audio. Commit the new files in `public/audio/`. `audio:dry` shows what would be recorded and the character count. `audio:check` checks for gaps. `audio:samples` renders candidate voices into `.voice-samples/`, which is gitignored. Mute is saved in `localStorage`.
- **Illustration kit (`hazard-kit.js`, `hazard-kit.css`), the "Public Information Cartoon":** both tests draw their clips from one kit, loaded after `hazard.js` and before the test's own script. The test script builds the clips first (`HazardKit.build(root, clips)`, which needs each clip's `dur` and `cue`), then calls `HazardTest.init`. Rules and palette roles are in DESIGN.md under "Hazard test illustration". Every clip in both pools is drawn by the kit; the old top-down dots and `bm-*` scenery are gone.
  - **Cast:** `CAST` in the kit, ids `c1`–`c12` (customers) and `s1`–`s4` (staff), each with a fixed skin, hair, top, build and height. In the customer test "you" is always `c12`. Neutral ids, no gender. Tops are never ink or bar wood (they vanish on the floor and counter).
  - **Set and cameras:** `<div class="hk-set" data-set="room">` is the room seen from behind the bar (staff-side camera, `data-cam="sc"`: back wall, door, WC door, lamps, pool table, a table, the counter nearest you). `data-set="bar"` is the back bar seen from the room (customer-side camera, `data-cam="cc"`: shelves, mirror, taps, till, the counter). The room's upper wall carries flat decor (frames, a chalkboard, a dartboard, lamps) so the space above the heads isn't dead; the floor line is at 44% and the counter at 76%, which keeps people at the counter filling the middle of the frame. Put `data-cam` on the shot (or on a `.hk-cut` block). Lanes give depth: `sc` lanes 0 (at the counter) to 3 (back wall) plus `f` (a colleague on your side of the counter, seen from behind, feet below the frame); `cc` lanes 0 (at the counter, seen from behind), 1 to 3 (each nearer the camera: a queue running toward the camera) and `s` (staff behind the counter). `LANES` maps a lane to feet height, scale and z-index. The wall colour is `--wall` on `.hpt-stage` (pink in the customer test, blue in the barperson test, set in each test's CSS).
  - **Figures:** `<i class="hk-person" data-cast="c7" data-view="front|back" data-pos="70,0" data-act="...">`. `data-pos` is `x,lane` where the figure ends up. `data-you` adds the cream YOU pill. A bubble for that speaker goes inside the element: `<p class="hk-bub" data-side="l|r" data-on="2300-5500">…</p>`. Your own lines in the barperson test are `<p class="hk-bub me" style="--x:30" data-on="5300-6700">` as direct children of the shot.
  - **Acting (`data-act`):** `"t: token token; t: token"` in ms. Pose tokens (see `POSES`): `idle`, `bar` (hands on the counter), `lean`, `wave` (tenner up), `point-r`/`point-l`/`point-rl`/`point-lr`/`point-rd`/`point-ld`, `phone`, `carry`, `hold-r(pint|stout|gin|note|card|phone|reader|mitten|point|open)`, `hold-l(…)`, `pour`, `reader`, `card` (card at the hip), `tap` (card raised out to a reader), `open-r`, `shrug`, `cup`, `sip`, `tilt`, `brow-r`/`brow-l`/`brows`/`brows-0`/`frown`, `smile`/`mouth-o`/`mouth-line`/`mouth-wide`, `look-l`/`look-r`/`look-ll`/`look-rr`/`look-cam`/`look-up`/`look-down`/`look-dl`/`look-dr`, `face-l`/`face-r`/`face-c` (three-quarter turn). Movement: `at(x,lane)` jumps there; `go(x,lane)` walks there; `hide`/`show` (260 ms fade), `vanish`/`appear` (instant), `legs`/`nolegs`; `turn-back`/`turn-front` turn one figure round (both views are built; the face slides 200 ms before the swap). Hands: `hold-r(pint|stout|gin|note|card|phone|reader|mitten|point|open)` and `hold-l(…)` (a belly grip); `cue-r`/`cue-l` hold a pool cue at the side, clear of the face. Loops with a length in ms: `wave(ms)`, `hover(ms)`, `glance(ms)`, `drum(ms)`; `nod` is one two-dip nod; `talk(ms)` is compiled into the mouth tracks (the mouth and an "o" alternate every 130 ms), so it costs no animation. A pose only changes the parts it names; everything else holds. The kit compiles each part's changes into one `@keyframes` (percentages from the clip's `dur`) so the player can seek it, and starts the loops with inline delays and iteration counts (loops are neutral at both ends and run with `fill-mode: none`, so several can stack on one part).
  - **Walks (`go`):** speed comes from the distance and the character's pace. Each cast member has a stride period from `HazardKit.pace(cast)` (380–560 ms: taller and broader regulars longer, grey-haired ones slower), a stride covers 0.6 of the figure's height (0.4 when carrying), and the walk is always a whole number of strides, so a walker ends with both feet down. `go(x,lane,ms)` makes the time an override (still rounded to whole strides); `pace(ms)` before a `go` sets a hurried or lazy pace for that figure from then on. The gait is picked from the direction: a side-on waddle (body rock, hop, leg lifts), a towards/away walk (shoulder sway, lift, the depth scale changing each step) or a careful carrying walk (arms locked on the props, short steps, less bob). The figure travels while a foot is up and stops as it lands, faces the way it is going during a sideways walk, counter-swings its arms (not when carrying), squashes on landing, and the head and any bun lag the body a little. With the legs hidden (behind the counter) the shoulder rock, head and arms still say "walking". Loops nobody could see are left out (no leg lifts when the legs are hidden for the whole walk, no forearm trail or head lag on far figures, no arm swing on tiny ones), so a full-size walk costs about 9 animations and a far one about 3. The kit warns in the console if a walk crosses the cue or the end of the clip; never leave a figure mid-stride on either frame. The YOU pill and any bubbles inside a figure counter-scale with its live lane scale, so they stay the same size as it walks between lanes.
  - **Background life:** front-view figures do small seeded things when nothing else is asked of them (a glance, a turn to a friend, a raised brow, a sip if they hold a pint), never during the freeze, a walk or within 600 ms of a scripted act. `data-life="0"` turns it off for a figure.
  - **Props and timing:** `<i class="hk-prop" data-prop="pint|stout|cider|gin|bottle|glass|glass-stout|glass-gin|glass-cider|note|card|reader" style="--x:50;--y:50;--w:3.6" data-on="8800">` sits on the counter (`--y` is its base). `data-on="a-b"` on any element shows it from a to b (bubbles pop with a spring; `.hk-cut` blocks switch instantly). `data-move="t: x,y[,s]; …"` slides a prop to another spot (500 ms). The reader shows `data-screen="1500:£11.40; 2400:spin; 7400:ok; 9000:off"`. Pouring is under "The back bar" below. Set pieces (`hk-table`, `hk-pool`, `hk-frame`, `hk-chalk`, `hk-dart`, `hk-lamp`) can be placed anywhere in a shot with `--x`/`--y` (give one a `z-index` to sit in front of a lane, e.g. a table in front of lane 3).
  - **Hard cuts:** `<div class="hk-cut" data-cam="sc" data-on="5400-7000">` holds a second camera's set and figures and shows it for that window (no more than two cuts per clip). Mirror x positions between cameras (x becomes 100 − x).
  - **The back bar and the turn-round (barperson test):** a shot holds a 200%-wide strip, `<div class="hk-pan" data-pan="0:0; 2500:-50; 6150:0">`, with two `.hk-view` panes: the room (`data-cam="sc"`, `data-set="room"`, the customers) and the back bar (`data-set="back"`). Each turn starts at its time (put the `whoosh` there) and takes 650 ms; `-50` shows the back bar, `0` the room. The back set draws the wall, shelves, the mirror, the tap font (four taps at stage x 35, 45, 55 and 65: cream, ink, light blue, cream knobs), the drip tray (glasses stand on it at `--y:80`), the back counter and the till. **Mirror:** figures placed as children of the back set are moved into the mirror automatically (`data-cam="mirror"`): give them the same x and lane they have in the room, and the flip puts them on the correct side; walks (`go`) work there too, in mirror space. **Pouring:** `<i class="hk-prop" data-prop="glass|glass-stout|glass-gin|glass-cider" style="--x:45;--y:80;--w:4.6;--f:0.75" data-fill="3400" data-fillms="1800" data-head="5200" data-settle="5200" data-settlems="3000" data-top="10800">`: `data-fill` starts the pour at that ms and fills to `--f` of the glass over `data-fillms`; a stout surges tan and `data-settle` turns it dark from the bottom up to the same line over `data-settlems` (default 1400); `data-head` shows a thin head band that sits on the liquid line; `data-top` raises body and head to the rim in 600 ms. A lager is `data-fill` plus `data-head`; a gin gets ice cubes and `data-fizz="ms"` bubbles. `<i class="hk-stream" style="--x:45;--c:var(--wood)" data-on="3400-5200">` draws the stream from the nozzle while the tap is pulled. Full static drinks: `pint`, `stout`, `cider`, `gin`, `bottle`. **Your arm:** `<i class="hk-arm1p" data-hand="mitten|point|open" style="--x:46;--y:49;--skin:#e0ac69;--side:1" data-on="2500-6000" data-wave="2600-3400">` reaches up into frame from the bottom (from the left with `--side:1`, the right with `-1`; keep the sleeve off the glasses), the hand landing at (`--x`,`--y`), which for a tap handle is x 35/45/55/65, y 49; `data-wave` wiggles it for a beckon. **Tap pull:** `data-pull="2:2500-6000; 2:10900-11450"` on the back set tilts tap 2 for those windows (tap 2 is the stout tap, with a cream band). **Mirror coordinates:** the mirror box is at stage left 24%, top 2%, width 52%, height 44%, flipped, so a mirror-space point (mx, my) is at stage x = 24 + 52 × (1 − mx/100), y = 2 + 44 × my/100 (use that for a freeze on the back view). Reflections get no breathing, blinking or background life. Your own lines stay `.hk-bub.me` children of the shot, outside the strip, so they survive the turn. Sound cues to match: `whoosh` at each turn, `surge` as a stout starts, `pour` for lagers, `fizz` for a gin, `clink` when a drink lands.
  - **The pundit freeze:** `<div class="hk-freeze" data-at="60,44" data-zoom="1.3" data-ring="66,30" data-r="9" data-sight="66,31 50,59" data-stamp="the look">` as the shot's last child. `data-at` is the push-in centre, `data-ring` the circle (defaults to `data-at`), `data-r` its radius in % of the stage width, `data-sight` one or more `x1,y1 x2,y2` gaze lines (pre-push coordinates, `;`-separated), `data-bracket` the same for a dashed measuring bracket with end ticks (the Hoverer's metre), `data-stamp` the situation, never the answer (it renders as "hazard: the look" and is the clip's only hazard label; the player's own badge is hidden on kit clips by CSS), `data-stamp-at` the corner the clip keeps clear of faces, hands, the hazard prop and the YOU pill: `tl` (default), `tr`, `bl` or `br`. The push-in starts 850 ms before the cue, the scrim and circle land by the cue, and everything lifts 400 ms after it. Below a 500px-wide stage the scrim darkens to 60% and the ring and sightline thicken (a container query in `hazard-kit.css`).
  - **Authoring a clip, step by step:** (1) write the beat, camera, cast, gestures and cue moment in one line each; (2) copy a rebuilt shot, set `data-cam` and the set; (3) place the figures with `data-pos` and give each a `data-act` script in clip time; (4) add bubbles inside their speakers, your own lines at the bottom, props with `data-on` at the sound cues; (5) add the freeze with the ring on the hazard and a sightline if gaze matters; (6) keep the sound cue times in the test's JS in step and update the clip's `label`; (7) review with `?clips=N` at 1280 and 390 wide, including with reduced motion, and check the cue and end frames read on their own. Budget: about 120 running animations per clip (the kit reports none; count with `shot.getAnimations({ subtree: true }).length`).
  - **Audio scripts:** `scripts/lib/hazard-lines.mjs` runs each test's JS in a sandbox with `HazardKit` stubbed and skips `hazard-kit.js`.
- **Behaviour:** after an answer the verdict shows with a "Next clip" button. The outcome plays and holds on its last frame until the viewer presses it (play at that point replays the outcome). Next cuts off any narration still playing. The player pauses when scrolled out of view or the tab is hidden. With reduced motion it jumps straight to each question and straight to the end of each outcome. Answers can also be keyed with 1–4 or A–D.
- **Certification badge (`hazard-badge.js`, `hazard-badge.css`, `badges/`):** a perfect run (every dealt clip right) earns that test's certificate: "full licence" (customer, Pink Gin) or "landlord material" (barperson, Quiz Night Blue). When a test ends, `finish()` in `hazard.js` dynamic-imports `hazard-badge.js` and calls `result({ root, panel, score, n })`. That's the player's only hook. Below a perfect run the module adds one nudge line and nothing else. On a perfect run it loads `hazard-badge.css` and puts the certificate in the result panel, above "Take it again": the filled master as inline SVG, an optional "Name on the licence" field (24 characters), and the actions. "Share your licence" (Web Share with the PNG, only where `navigator.canShare({ files })` says yes, so mostly phones) is the one amber button, or "Save the image" where there's no share sheet. "Copy link" copies the share line plus the test URL. "Take it again" drops to an outline. The stamp reveal (card rises, the seal drops on it, about 1.2s) plays once, when the badge is half on screen, and is skipped with reduced motion. The fields `#f-name`, `#f-date` (en-GB, "8 Oct 2026"), `#f-number` (`DQ-1000` to `DQ-9999`, random per award) and `#f-site` (the master's domain on localhost and `*.workers.dev`, otherwise the real host) are filled through `textContent`, so XMLSerializer escapes `<`, `&` and quotes. Each one shrinks from `data-max-size` towards `data-min-size` until it fits `data-max-width`, then is cut with an ellipsis. An empty name shows the master's `data-default` ("a regular", "whoever’s on tonight"). The PNG is a 1080x1080 canvas drawn from a `data:` copy of the SVG. CSP allows `data:` images but not `blob:` ones, so `blob:` is only used for the download link. The name never leaves the device and isn't stored. Inline, the master's ids get an `aw-` prefix (`#aw-seal`). The masters must stay self-contained: no external files, `<image>`, `<foreignObject>` or web fonts, a system font stack on every live `<text>`, and fixed words as outlined paths. Their generator (`masters.py`), the landscape 1200x630 versions and the design spec live with the design work, not in this repo. **To add a certificate for a new test,** add one entry to `BADGES` at the top of `hazard-badge.js`, keyed by the page's file name without `.html` (for example `hazard-landlord`), and put its square master in `badges/`. The test page needs no changes.
- **Clip preview (`/hazard-preview`):** a staff page listing every clip in every test's pool, with its timing, label, question, options (right one in green) and a `?clips=N` link into the real player, plus "Play all N in order". It is unlinked and `noindex`, so don't link it or add it to a sitemap or `robots.txt`. It needs no upkeep: the tests come from the hub's `a.hz-door` links, in hub order, and the clips come from each test's config, read fresh on every load. For each test it fetches the page, then runs its `<script src>` list in order, minus `hazard.js`, in a blank hidden iframe whose stub `HazardTest.init` records the config. It warns when a page's `.hpt-shot` count doesn't match `clips.length`. The only rule: each test's scripts must still call `HazardTest.init` with the full config when loaded without a stage (no `#hpt`, no templates, nothing on screen).

## Backend

- **Database:** Neon Postgres (eu-west-2), table `signatures` (`id`, `name`, `email`, `consent`, `consent_at`, `created_at`). The unique index on `lower(email)` means duplicate emails are silently ignored, so the API doesn't reveal who has signed.
- **Hyperdrive:** id `88c5a2e2902f470995344cd16411c3fc` in the current Cloudflare account (the earlier account had `bar-petition`, id `7918ba51…`, which is no longer used). Use Neon's **direct** (non-`-pooler`) host. Hyperdrive does the pooling. It is bound as `HYPERDRIVE` in `wrangler.jsonc`.
- **Worker:** `src/worker.js` checks the Origin header, has a honeypot field `website`, validates name, email and consent, and caches `/api/count` for 60s. `/api/*` and `/` run the Worker first (`run_worker_first`), and so does any path with no matching file (it returns the 404 from `env.ASSETS`). Everything else is served straight from `public/`.
- **Secrets:** the connection string is stored in Hyperdrive only. It is not in the repo. Never commit it.

## Gotchas

- **Hyperdrive error 10157 (config not found):** the id in `wrangler.jsonc` must exist in the account Wrangler is logged into. If `wrangler deploy` fails with 10157, you are probably logged into the wrong account (`npx wrangler whoami`), or the id changed. Check with `npx wrangler hyperdrive list`. `wrangler dev` may rewrite the id.
- **Old account leftovers:** the previous personal account may still host `dont-queue-at-the-bar` and the older `stop-queueing-at-the-bar` Workers, plus the `bar-petition` Hyperdrive config. They are unused. Clean them up there if you still have access.
- **Testing animations:** In an automated or hidden browser tab, scroll events and IntersectionObservers don't fire reliably. Force them by dispatching a `scroll` event or adding `in` to `.reveal` elements.
- **Count endpoint:** the first call after a deploy once returned a Cloudflare 1042 error, then worked. Watch for it.
