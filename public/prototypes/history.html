a short history

Blame 2020. Mostly.

over five years

since you were allowed back at the bar. Still queueing.

Nobody told anyone to queue at the bar. The 2020 rules said not to gather at it, pubs reopened to stickers and table service, and the line outlasted the rules. Eleven stops from 1996 to today, each with a source, and still quicker than the queue.

  1. before2020

    The invisible queue.

    “The bar counter is the only place in Britain in which anything is sold or served without the formation of a queue.”

    Kate Fox, Passport to the Pub, 1996

  2. 20 Mar2020

    Pubs shut.

    The Prime Minister asks pubs to close ‘tonight as soon as they reasonably can’. Ordering at the bar won’t be rule-free again for 486 days.

    GOV.UK, PM statement, 20 Mar 2020

  3. 4 Jul2020

    Pubs reopen. Carefully.

    Table service where possible. Stickers on the floor. Don’t gather at the bar. The rules never said ‘queue at the bar’. Somewhere in here, people started to.

    GOV.UK guidance for pubs, 2020 (later revision)

  4. 24 Sep2020

    Table service. By law.

    Sit down. Order from your seat. Home by ten. For 298 days, the bar is something you look at. Lockdowns permitting.

    SI 2020/1029 (England), 24 Sep 2020

  5. 19 Jul2021

    The bar is back.

    The rules go at 11.55pm the night before. You can walk up to the bar again. Legally. Nobody tells the queue.

    SI 2021/848 (England), 19 Jul 2021

  6. 30 Jul2021

    The habit sticks.

    Eleven days after the rules go, YouGov asks how you’d rather order. From your table, 51%. At the bar, 25%. Table service isn’t a queue, but it is the habit of not walking to the bar. It stuck.

    YouGov, 30 Jul 2021, 5,695 adults

  7. 28 Jan2022

    Last orders for table service.

    Wales drops table service, the last of the four nations to. 679 days after the pubs shut.

    Welsh Government, 28 Jan 2022

  8. July2023

    Someone notices.

    Time Out asks: ‘Why have people started queuing single-file in pubs?’ Two years after the rules ended.

    Time Out, July 2023

  9. 23 Aug2025

    America notices.

    The Wall Street Journal calls the single-file queue ‘a mortal threat’ to British pub culture. Two years after Time Out. Same question, bigger newspaper.

    The Wall Street Journal, 23 Aug 2025

  10. 18 Sep2025

    Britain splits.

    YouGov asks which you’d prefer. Single file, 40%. Along the bar, 39%. Don’t know, 21%. One point in it. One in four adults had seen a single-file bar queue that year.

    YouGov, 18 Sep 2025, 5,914 GB adults

  11. today

    over five years since you were allowed back at the bar.

    Still queueing. That’s long enough.

    Counted from 19 Jul 2021, England (SI 2021/848)

questions

Asked and answered.

Why do people queue at the bar now?

Nobody told them to. The 2020 guidance said not to gather at the bar at all; floor markings were for entrances and queues outside. Pubs reopened to stickers and table service, and the line outlasted the rules. 2020 normalised it; it didn’t invent it.

GOV.UK guidance for pubs, 2020

When did table service end in pubs?

England, 19 July 2021. Wales was the last of the four, on 28 January 2022.

SI 2021/848 (England) · Welsh Government, 28 Jan 2022

Do most people prefer queueing at the bar?

Depends who asks. YouGov, September 2025: single file 40%, along the bar 39%, don’t know 21%. A trade survey for Zonal and CGA, December 2025: along the bar 69%, a line 31%.

YouGov, 18 Sep 2025, 5,914 GB adults · Zonal and CGA by NIQ, GO Technology, Dec 2025, 5,000 consumers

Is it rude to go to the bar when there’s a queue?

Not at a bar, which Kate Fox called the only place in Britain where anything is served without a queue. The invisible queue polices itself: in 2017, 91% told YouGov they’d point out that someone else was next if they were served out of turn.

Kate Fox, Passport to the Pub, 1996 · YouGov, 24 Nov 2017

How much slower is the single file?

Twenty orders through one spot on the bar take 25 minutes at 75 seconds each. Across four staff, six. Arithmetic, not a stopwatch.

A worked example, not a survey. Illustrative, like the sims on the home page.

How do you order without queueing?

Walk to the bar. Pick a gap. Catch an eye. Trust the barperson. The four steps are on the home page, and there is a test.
