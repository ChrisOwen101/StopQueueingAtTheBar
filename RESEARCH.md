## Derived figures (arithmetic, shown so anyone can check it)

Dates (calendar days, end date exclusive):

- **486 days**: pubs told to close (20 Mar 2020) to bar ordering rule-free in England (19 Jul 2021).
- **298 days**: table service compulsory in England (24 Sep 2020) to 19 Jul 2021. Pubs were shut by lockdowns for much of it, hence “lockdowns permitting” on the page.
- **1,911 days**: 19 Jul 2021 to 12 Oct 2026. The page counts this live.
- Also true, not on the page: in those 16 months English pubs could open indoors on at most **222 days** (4 Jul to 5 Nov 2020: 124; 2 Dec 2020 to 6 Jan 2021: 35, tier-dependent; 17 May to 19 Jul 2021: 63). Table service was the law on about 140 of the days pubs were actually open indoors. Wales dropped table service **679 days** after the pubs closed.

The maths screen (a worked example, labelled “Simple arithmetic, not a survey” and “Illustrative, like the sims” on the page):

- Twenty orders at 75 seconds each, through one spot on the bar: 20 × 75 s = 1,500 s = **25 minutes**.
- The same twenty orders across four staff: 20 ÷ 4 × 75 s = 375 s = **6¼ minutes**.
- One spot on the bar can serve 3,600 ÷ 75 = **48 orders an hour**. Against 90 an hour on a Friday night the line grows by about 42 people an hour.
- Why 75 seconds: it is generous to the single file. Anglia Ruskin’s pubLAB observed an average of 2 min 23 s per customer from first contact to leaving with a drink (2015). At that rate the twenty take about 48 minutes through one spot and 12 across four.
- The honest caveat, which the page’s wording follows: a single queue that feeds *every* member of staff (the post office “cashier number three, please”) is efficient. The problem is that a single file at a bar ends at one spot, so in practice one or two staff serve it.

## Background: the queueing theory (not on the page)

Standard M/M/c queue models (Larson and Odoni, *Urban Operations Research*, MIT, ch. 4, https://web.mit.edu/urban_or_book/www/book/index.html; Erlang C; Little’s law, J.D.C. Little, *Operations Research* 9(3), 1961). Assumptions: four staff, 75 s per order on average, random arrivals and service times. A single file ending at one spot is modelled as one server; people spread along the bar as four.

| Demand | One serving point | Two | Four |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet, 36 orders an hour | average wait 3 min 45 s | 12 s | 0.2 s |
| Steady, 45 an hour | 18 min 45 s, about 14 waiting | 21 s | 0.4 s |
| Friday rush, 90 an hour | never clears: the line grows by 42 an hour | 9 min 4 s | 5 s |

Real service times vary less than these models assume, so real waits are shorter, but the gap between one serving point and four stays huge. The page uses the plain arithmetic instead, because “a wait under a second” invites ridicule and the models need more explaining than a beer mat allows.

## The wider timeline (for reference)

England: pubs told to close 20 Mar 2020 (law from 2pm 21 Mar, SI 2020/327); “one metre plus” announced 23 Jun 2020; reopening 4 Jul 2020 (SI 2020/684), bar ordering discouraged but legal; table service and a 10pm curfew compulsory from 5am 24 Sep 2020 (SI 2020/1029); tiers from 14 Oct 2020; lockdown 5 Nov to 2 Dec 2020; lockdown from 6 Jan 2021; outdoor hospitality, seated, from 12 Apr 2021; indoor from 17 May 2021, with table service kept (“COVID-19 Response – Spring 2021”, CP 398); everything revoked at 11.55pm 18 Jul 2021 (SI 2021/848).

Scotland: outdoor 6 Jul 2020, indoor 15 Jul 2020; beyond Level 0 on 9 Aug 2021; table service back for Omicron 27 Dec 2021 to 24 Jan 2022 (gov.scot).

Wales: outdoor 13 Jul 2020, indoor 3 Aug 2020; alert level 0 on 7 Aug 2021; table service back from Boxing Day 2021; dropped 28 Jan 2022, the last UK nation (gov.wales).

Northern Ireland: pubs serving food 3 Jul 2020, others 23 Sep 2020; indoor 24 May 2021; standing to drink from 31 Oct 2021; table service back over Christmas 2021 and dropped at noon on 21 Jan 2022 (Executive Office).

**Two nuances the copy must keep:**

1. The guidance never told anyone to queue single file *at the bar*. It said not to gather at the bar at all; floor markings were for entrances and queues outside.
2. COVID normalised the bar queue; it didn’t invent it. Bar queues were reported before 2020 (Boak and Bailey, “Queuing in Pubs: Feels So Wrong, But So Right”, Aug 2018; an Edinburgh pub’s “lager lane”, Morning Advertiser, Jun 2019).

## Avoid

- The YouGov 40/39 poll presented as a 2026 poll. It is from 18 Sep 2025.
- The regional split (London 37%, Midlands/North/Wales 42%). It appears in only one secondary source (All Access London, 21 Aug 2026).
- “COVID invented the bar queue.” Say it normalised it.
- “The government told us to queue single file at the bar.” It told us not to gather at the bar.
- Calling the maths screen “Little’s law” or “random arrivals”. It is plain arithmetic for a fixed group of twenty.
- “No queue. Ever.” before 2020. Bar queues were reported before the pandemic (see the nuances above).
- “486 days before you could order at the bar.” Ordering at the bar was legal, if discouraged, from 4 Jul to 23 Sep 2020; the page says “won’t be rule-free again for 486 days”.
- Laws quoted as UK-wide. The SIs are England’s; the page says so, and notes that Wales was last.
- “Brits spend two months of their life waiting at the bar.” DataSparQ marketing, 2019, no method.
- **Hackney Church Brew Co: deliberately unused.** The “+25% like-for-like” (from the managing director’s own Broadsheet op-ed, which itself says “One Thursday doesn’t constitute a scientific study”) is the brewery’s claim, and the Evening Standard reported 20%. “Nearly three times as long” (Metro, 19 Aug 2026) is an assertion with no data. Its pint price, bar length and staff count beyond the post’s “four members of staff” were not verified.
- **The East London Times article** (29 Aug 2026) that earlier docs cited could not be found anywhere. Do not cite it.
- @QueuesPub’s follower counts and founding year (the WSJ says 2022; the first dated evidence is July 2023). Naming and shaming pubs isn’t our tone anyway.
- The “Super Saturday” and “Freedom Day” nicknames (not checked).
- Tottenham’s “10,000 pints a minute”, “200 drinks an hour” per member of bar staff and any average UK pub floor area or bar length (no source exists).
- Flipdish’s “80% of pub-goers want to order on apps” (the company’s own claim).
