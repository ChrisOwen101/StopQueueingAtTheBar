# Posters (notice family), round 2: marketing notes

Reviewed 14 Oct 2026 against the A4 and A5 renders and the tenths in this folder, with `../round-1/marketing.md` and `../round-1/decision.md`. Judged on message, hierarchy and behaviour, not letterforms.

## The pair holds

The notice is the landlord's sheet and the instruction poster is the taproom's, and seen side by side they teach the grammar (column, no; row, yes) without a word. Nothing in this round changes that. Three decisions were open; here is where I land on each.

## The notice's field: ink

Ink. From the door the ink sheet says three things in order: NO QUEUEING, the red "no" sign, then the white house line; the red sheet says them all at once, in one shout. More to the point, the ink one looks like a notice the brewery sent and the red one looks like a poster the campaign sent, and the whole reason for this family is that a landlord puts up the first kind without a second thought. The red ring on ink is also the one symbol on any wall that needs no reading.

The red field had a case (it is the Single File's colour, and it uses less ink), but the colour sheet is for a colour printer or the print shop either way; the office prints the mono, and the mono is the same sheet whichever field we pick.

## The copy

- **The polite ask** needs a fix and the designer has it: "We kindly ask that you spread out. / The bar staff know who’s next." Keep "We kindly ask": it is the register of the reference and half the joke. "Along the bar" is already in the headline and in the picture.
- **"This bar does The Cluster."** stays as the house line. It is the pub's pledge and the drinker's permission, as the flyer settled.
- **The stock code** stays. "DQ/POS/BAR-001" is for the bar staff, who decide whether a sign survives the week.
- **The subhead** on the instruction poster is "It’s a pub. Not a post office." and it is finally in the right place: the second read, under the instruction, where it explains the name. At 600 it reads from further, which is the point of a subhead on a wall.
- **"FIND / THE / GAP."** is a good second headline for the same sheet. Offer it as an alternate if the team wants two instruction posters; the Tube echo lands with anyone who has lived in a city with a metro and reads as a cheerful instruction to everyone else. It carries "Take the test." as its scan reason, which suits it: the person told to find a gap is the one who wants to know if they'd pass.

## Which to ship

- **The notice, mono** (the office version) and **the notice, colour** (ink). The mono first on the download page, with one line: "Colour sheets are for a colour printer or a print shop; the black-and-white ones are for the office laser."
- **The instruction poster, green**, colour and mono. "Hang them together": the notice behind the bar facing the room, the poster by the end of the bar where the line forms.
- The alternate headline as a third download only if the team wants it; otherwise it stays in the script's data for a pub that asks.

## What this family doesn't replace

The flyers. Face A is the campaign's poster in the campaign's voice with the stamp leading; Face B is the how-to. These are the pub's notices in the pub's register. The download page can offer both sets under one heading ("Posters. Print your own."), the flyers for the campaign's voice and the notices for the house's, and the notes above about ink go on the page, not the sheets.
