# Round 4: naming, taglines and the invisible queue

Marketing and copy research, 13 Oct 2026. Answers the feedback that the name and logo read as "don't queue at all" or "no queue, barge in". The campaign is against **The Single File**. The right behaviour, **The Cluster**, is still a queue. People spread along the bar and the bar staff keep the order in their heads. They know who's next. They always did.

Nothing here has been registered, checked with Companies House or searched on social media. The domain checks below did not run (see section 3).

## 1. Diagnosis

**Why "Don't Queue At The Bar" misfires**

1. It is a prohibition with no replacement. "Don't queue" leaves a hole, and the reader fills it with the only other thing they know: pushing in. A negative name needs the positive to follow it, and nothing in the name does.
2. "At the bar" is where both behaviours happen. The name says stop doing the thing in that place. It doesn't say do the thing differently. The real instruction is about the shape of the queue, not its existence.
3. In Britain the queue is sacred. A campaign that sounds anti-queue sounds rude, and the joke lands on manners instead of on the post-office line. We want the single file to be the punchline, not politeness.
4. The roundel has no words, by design. On its own it shows three people spread along a bar, which is right. Beside "don't queue" the same picture reads as "crowd the bar". The name is colouring the mark.
5. The mat front is the stamp: the name alone, in a ring, no tagline. So the one object that will sit in front of a stranger in a pub says "don't queue at the bar" with nothing to soften or explain it. That's where the misreading was always going to happen.

**What the name has to do**

- Keep the word "queue", or an obvious stand-in for order. We're for queueing. We're against the line.
- Say the change of shape: sideways, along, wide, spread. That is the whole instruction.
- Leave "who's next" to the tagline, or at least not contradict it. The virtual queue is the barperson's job, and the name must not sound like we've abolished it.
- Be sayable as an instruction, by a stranger or a barperson, in a pub, in two seconds. The best name is one bar staff can say out loud: "queue sideways, please."
- Fit the system: two lowercase lines of up to 11 characters for the 40px lockup, a ring stamp for the mat, and a domain you can read off a beer mat without squinting.

## 2. Twelve candidates

Line counts are for the two-line lowercase wordmark. The stamp needs the same words in a ring at 48px and up. "Existing uses" means ones known to me; none have been searched.

### 1. Don't Queue At The Bar (kept; tagline does the fixing)

- **Tagline:** Along it. Not at it.
- **Domain:** dontqueueatthebar.co.uk (in use), dontqueueatthebar.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Don't queue at the bar." "What, just push in?" "No. Along it, not at it." Needs the second line every time. The name on its own loses the exchange.
- **Gets right:** nothing about the single file or the virtual queue. The tagline rescues it by moving the stress from "queue" to "at": the problem is queueing at one point of the bar instead of along the length of it.
- **Risks:** the name still says "don't queue". The stamp (mat front) carries the name and no tagline, so the mat would need the tagline added or the badge-round front instead. The roundel beside "don't" still reads as a crowd. Every share, screenshot and overheard mention drops the tagline. Lockup: "don't queue / at the bar." (11 / 11), already fits. No domain change.

### 2. Queue Sideways

- **Tagline:** Same queue. Turned ninety degrees.
- **Domain:** queuesideways.co.uk, queuesideways.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Queue sideways." Said with a flat hand sweeping along the bar. Understood in one go, and funny because it sounds like a dance step. It also works as a thing bar staff say: "Queue sideways, please, there's room."
- **Gets right:** keeps "queue", so nobody thinks we're anti-queue. "Sideways" is the shape change in one word: take the single file and lay it along the bar. The order is untouched, so the virtual queue is implied, and the tagline makes it explicit.
- **Risks:** "sideways" also means drunk ("it went sideways"), which in a pub campaign is a feature. Could be read as sidling in at the side of the queue; the roundel kills that, because it pictures three people spread out, not one sneaking round. No existing uses known. Lockup: "queue / sideways." (5 / 9). Domain 13 characters. Works as a hashtag.

### 3. Queue Along The Bar

- **Tagline:** It's a pub. Not a post office.
- **Domain:** queuealongthebar.co.uk, queuealongthebar.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Queue along the bar." Plain. Nobody can misread it. The least funny of the twelve, but the clearest, and the YouGov question used the same words ("along the bar"), so it's already how people describe it.
- **Gets right:** keeps "queue". "Along" is the exact opposite of a single file running away from the bar. The roundel is a literal diagram of the name.
- **Risks:** said quickly, "along the bar" and "at the bar" sound close, so the stress needs to be on "along". Four words is a lot on a mat ring. Flat as a joke: the comedy has to come from the tagline and the page. Lockup: "queue along / the bar." (11 / 8). Domain 16 characters.

### 4. The Bar Is The Queue

- **Tagline:** Stand along it. They know who's next.
- **Domain:** thebaristhequeue.co.uk, thebaristhequeue.uk
- **Stranger test:** "The bar is the queue." A beat, then they get it. It's a riddle that answers itself, and it sticks. Works as a chant and as a sign behind the bar.
- **Gets right:** the strongest statement of the virtual queue in any candidate. It says the queue exists, it's the bar itself, and your place in it is held by the person behind it. Nothing to abolish, nothing to barge.
- **Risks:** needs that beat, and some people won't give it one. Could read as "the bar is a queue" (i.e. a complaint). Four small words set in a ring look like a sentence, not a name. Lockup: "the bar is / the queue." (10 / 10). Domain 16 characters. Read fast, the domain starts "thebarist…", so someone will see a barista in it. Minor, but someone will spot it.

### 5. No Single File

- **Tagline:** Queue along the bar. They know who's next.
- **Domain:** nosinglefile.co.uk, nosinglefile.uk
- **Stranger test:** "No single file." Clear once you know "single file" means the line. Out of context it sounds like a filing rule, a software term, or a dating site.
- **Gets right:** names the right enemy for the first time. It's against the line, not the queue. Pairs with the site's own term, The Single File.
- **Risks:** still a negative with no positive in it, so it inherits half the current problem. Sounds like an instruction on a form. The roundel beside a "no" reads as a crowd again, slightly. Lockup: "no single / file." (9 / 5), but "file" alone on a line looks wrong. Domain 12 characters.

### 6. Spread Along The Bar

- **Tagline:** They know who's next. They always did.
- **Domain:** spreadalongthebar.co.uk, spreadalongthebar.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Spread along the bar." Understood at once. "Spread" invites a grin (toast, Marmite, "spread 'em"). Already the ring text on the badge-round, so it's half in use.
- **Gets right:** pure instruction, positive, and the picture on the roundel. Doesn't mention queueing at all, which cuts both ways.
- **Risks:** without "queue" in it, it can read as "fill the bar up" or "take up room", which is the barging reading by another route. Four words. Lockup: "spread along / the bar." (12 / 8), one over the limit; "spread / along the bar." (6 / 13) is worse. Domain 17 characters, the joint longest.

### 7. Don't Single File

- **Tagline:** Queue sideways. The barperson keeps count.
- **Domain:** dontsinglefile.co.uk, dontsinglefile.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Don't single file." Clunky in a British mouth; "single file" as a verb is American playground English. People will say "don't single-file what?"
- **Gets right:** the enemy is named. It's a direct swap of the wrong word in the current name ("queue" for "single file"), which is the exact correction the feedback asks for.
- **Risks:** the grammar. Still a prohibition with no positive. Easy to mishear as "don't single, file" or "don't, single file". Lockup: "don't single / file." (12 / 5), one over and "file" strands. Domain 14 characters.

### 8. Queue Wide

- **Tagline:** Queue wide. Not long.
- **Domain:** queuewide.co.uk, queuewide.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Queue wide." Two syllables. Sounds like a snooker instruction, which in a pub with a table is a bonus. The tagline completes it: wide, not long.
- **Gets right:** keeps "queue" and changes its shape in one short word. Cheapest to say, print and type.
- **Risks:** "wide" on its own is vague until the tagline arrives. "Wide boy". Sounds a little like a product name. A nine-character domain this clean is likely taken or parked. Lockup: "queue / wide." (5 / 5), the smallest of all, which may look thin beside the roundel at 64px. Domain 9 characters.

### 9. Along The Bar

- **Tagline:** Not down the room.
- **Domain:** alongthebar.co.uk, alongthebar.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Along the bar." Said like a direction. People already use this phrase for the old way of doing it, so it sounds like something they half remember.
- **Gets right:** the shape change in two words, positive, with the roundel as the picture. Reads as the name of a place and a way of standing.
- **Risks:** it's a phrase, not a name; it doesn't say "queue" and doesn't sound like a campaign. "Along The Bar" could be a gastropub. Lockup: "along / the bar." (5 / 7). Domain 11 characters.

### 10. They Know Who's Next

- **Tagline:** Stand along the bar. Leave the counting to them.
- **Domain:** theyknowwhosnext.co.uk, theyknowwhosnext.uk
- **Stranger test:** "They know who's next." Everyone who has ever stood at a bar nods. It's the truest line the campaign has. As a name it needs "who are they?", which the roundel answers.
- **Gets right:** the invisible queue, exactly. It's the barperson's skill in five words, and it flatters bar staff, who are the people who'll put the mats out.
- **Risks:** it's a tagline wearing a name's coat. Doesn't say what to do. Too long for the lockup: "they know / who's next." (9 / 10) fits, but the apostrophe at 40px is fiddly and the stamp ring is crowded. Domain 16 characters with an apostrophe lost. Best kept as the campaign's tagline, under any of the names.

### 11. Line The Bar

- **Tagline:** Line the bar. Not the room.
- **Domain:** linethebar.co.uk, linethebar.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Line the bar." Neat, and it turns the enemy's word ("line") into the instruction. Some people will hear something about cocaine. In a pub, they'll say so.
- **Gets right:** the shape change, positive, three short words, using the one word everyone already uses for the problem.
- **Risks:** the drugs reading is real and will be the first reply on social. "Line" is also the word for the thing we're against, so it muddies our own vocabulary (The Single File, the line). Lockup: "line / the bar." (4 / 7). Domain 10 characters.

### 12. The Invisible Queue

- **Tagline:** It's there. The barperson's got it.
- **Domain:** theinvisiblequeue.co.uk, theinvisiblequeue.uk
- **Stranger test:** "Join the invisible queue." Dry, and it gets a laugh, because it sounds like you're being had. Then it clicks: the queue is in the barperson's head.
- **Gets right:** the virtual queue is the name. It's the most on-brief answer to the feedback, and a good section heading or explainer title whatever we call the campaign.
- **Risks:** sounds like a complaint ("I've been in an invisible queue for ten minutes") or a ghost story. It doesn't tell anyone what to do. It clashes with the old ruling "The Phantom Queue". Lockup: "the invisible / queue." (13 / 5), over the limit. Domain 17 characters.

**Considered and dropped:** "Not A Post Office" (the tagline as a name; doesn't say what to do, and naming a campaign after the Post Office invites the Horizon scandal into every reply), "Join The Cluster" (an internal term; to a stranger it's a cult), "Queue Like It's 2019" (a date joke that ages by the week), "Stand Along The Bar" (fine, but "spread" and "queue" both beat "stand").

## 3. Domain availability

```sh
for d in queuesideways.co.uk queuesideways.uk; do
  printf '%s ' "$d"; curl -sS -m 10 -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' "https://rdap.nominet.uk/uk/domain/$d"
done
```

## 4. Ten taglines

For the hero and the mat. Each one explains the invisible queue in a breath. Typographic apostrophes, ready to paste.

1. It’s a pub. Not a post office. (current; stays)
2. They know who’s next. They always did.
3. Still a queue. Just not a line.
4. Same queue. Turned ninety degrees.
5. The queue is in the barperson’s head. Leave it there.
6. Nobody’s pushing in. The barperson keeps score.
7. The bar is twenty feet long. Use all of it.
8. One line, one barperson working. Spread out, all four.
9. Stand along the bar. Leave the counting to them.
10. Along it. Not at it.

Pairings that work as a two-line hero: 2 under any name; 3 or 4 under Queue Sideways; 10 under the current name; 1 under Queue Along The Bar, where the name is plain enough to carry a joke beneath it.

## 5. Shortlist

**1. Queue Sideways.** Tagline: *They know who’s next. They always did.* (hero), *Same queue. Turned ninety degrees.* (mat). Keeps "queue", so we're plainly for queueing. "Sideways" is the whole instruction in one word, and it's funny without trying. It's the only candidate bar staff can say out loud as a request, which is how the behaviour actually spreads. The roundel becomes its diagram. Fits the lockup with room to spare. **Needs a new domain** (queuesideways.co.uk and .uk), a new stamp, new mats, new OG images, new manifest name, new certificate ring text and a redirect from the current address. **This is the one I'd ship.**

**2. Queue Along The Bar.** Tagline: *It’s a pub. Not a post office.* The safe choice. Nobody can read it as "no queue" and nobody can read it as "barge in". It's how the public already describe the old way, so it needs no explaining. It's less catchy than Queue Sideways and gives the jokes less to push against. **Needs a new domain** (queuealongthebar.co.uk and .uk) and the same rebuild list as above.

**3. Don't Queue At The Bar, fixed by the tagline.** Tagline: *Along it. Not at it.* Keep the name and the domain, stress "at" and let the tagline turn it. Zero cost on domain, lockup, icons and the hazard seals. It's third because the name still says "don't queue" everywhere the tagline isn't: the stamp on the mat front, the favicon tooltip, a screenshot, someone repeating it in the pub. If we keep it, the mat front has to change to carry the tagline (or switch to the badge-round front with "spread along the bar" in the ring) and the OG description leads with the tagline. **No domain change.** Honest view: this is the fallback if the budget or the timing rules out a rename, not the answer to the feedback.

Near miss: **The Bar Is The Queue.** The best single line about the virtual queue, and a strong sign for behind the bar. It needs a beat to land, which a name can't ask for. Use it on the page as the heading of The Cluster section, under any of the three above.

## 6. Wordmark for the lockup

Two lines, lowercase, full stop on the second line as the stamp does now. Characters per line in brackets. The 11-character limit is what holds at 40px tall beside the roundel.

| Name | Line 1 | Line 2 | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Queue Sideways | `queue` (5) | `sideways.` (9) | yes, with room; consider the full stop after "sideways" and no stop on line 1 |
| Queue Along The Bar | `queue along` (11) | `the bar.` (8) | yes, at the limit on line 1 |
| Don't Queue At The Bar | `don’t queue` (11) | `at the bar.` (11) | yes, as now |

Notes for the stamp. Queue Sideways in the ring is two lines, which leaves more air than the current three; the ring may want to tighten or the type to grow a step. Queue Along The Bar in the ring is better as three lines ("queue / along / the bar."), the same shape as today's stamp. If the name changes, change it once in `scripts/lib/brand.mjs` and in `brand/final/build.mjs`, then re-run both build scripts and `npm run build:mats`, as AGENTS.md says. The roundel itself doesn't change for any of the three.
