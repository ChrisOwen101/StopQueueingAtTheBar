# Research: the landscape for a "stop the single file" identity

Round 4 brief research, 13 October 2026. Written to answer one piece of feedback: the current name and logo read as "don't queue at all". The identity has to say "stop the single file, not queueing", and it has to hint at the invisible queue.

**How this was researched, and the limits.** Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox's egress proxy for every news site tried (tovima.com, timeout.com, morningadvertiser.co.uk, britbrief.co.uk, theweek.com, petition.parliament.uk, eastlondontimes.co.uk and others all refused the connection). Everything below comes from web search result summaries, which carry short quoted passages from the pages. Quotes are therefore short, and where wording matters for copy, check it against the original before it goes on anything printed. Social handle availability could not be checked at all (X, Instagram and TikTok are not indexed by the search tool), so "nothing found" for a handle means only that. Trade mark registers were not queried directly. Two date notes: the YouGov poll in the project notes was fielded on 18 September 2025, not 2026, and the Hackney Church post is dated August 2026 in the project's own notes but the indexed coverage does not show its date.

## 1. Comparable campaigns and how they brand the idea

The short version: nobody in this space has a mark. Every existing effort is text, a photo, or a sign with words on it. The vocabulary is settled; the visual is a blank.

### @QueuesPub (since 2022)

- Run by schoolteacher Rod Truan. Started after Truan saw a single-file line at a pub in Cornwall. Readers send photos of queues, and the account posts them with the pub named. Reported as having "over 14,000 followers" (undated). Sources: [WSJ via To Vima](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/britains-pub-culture-faces-a-mortal-threat-the-single-file-queue), [Travel Tomorrow](https://traveltomorrow.com/to-queue-or-not-to-queue-what-is-the-right-way-to-get-served-in-british-pubs/).
- Bio: "A campaign to end the recent phenomenon of queuing single file in pubs. We queue for the bus or for the checkout, not at bars. Message in your photos." Sources: [LBC, July 2023](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/pubs-uk-british-queues-single-file-tradition-DWzcxz_2/), [Time Out](https://timeout.com/uk/news/why-have-people-started-queuing-single-file-in-pubs-073123).
- Truan to the Guardian, 2023: "It seems to have started because of Covid" and "It's like a hundred years of tradition have been swept away overnight." The case Truan makes for the right behaviour is social, not efficient: "when you go to the bar, you meet new people, you have conversations, you have banter". Sources: [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/pubs-uk-british-queues-single-file-tradition), [Time Out](https://timeout.com/uk/news/why-have-people-started-queuing-single-file-in-pubs-073123).
- Words for the right behaviour: "crowding around the bar and trying to catch the server's eye" (an Independent column describing the account). Source: [Independent via Yahoo](https://au.news.yahoo.com/voices-great-british-public-forgotten-103208423.html).
- Visuals: reader photos of lines, named pubs. No logo or mark is described anywhere in the coverage. The brand is the handle and the photos. Note the method is name and shame, which this campaign's principles rule out ("tease behaviour, never shame people or pubs", PRODUCT.md).
- LBC's 2023 headline for the story was "It's a pub not a post office!" ([LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/pubs-uk-british-queues-single-file-tradition)). The site's hero line "It's a pub. Not a post office." is therefore already common property, see below.

### Hackney Church Brew Co, "not a post office" (August 2026)

- Managing director Hamish Glenn told Metro the queue "is costing the venue business", that "queuing at the bar takes nearly three times as long", and that "if it takes a while to get your first drink, you are more reluctant to go up and buy a second". Source: [BritBrief, citing Metro](https://britbrief.co.uk/business/retail/london-brewery-begs-customers-to-stop-bar-queuing.html).
- The ask, in Glenn's words: "If you walk in and discover a single-file queue snaking away from the bar, do not join it. Walk directly to an empty bit of bar." And the permission slip: "You're not queue jumping. You're doing your bit to save British pub culture." Same source.
- The social post was a "public service announcement" asking people to "use the whole bar", complaining of "enormous stretches of completely empty bar". Glenn later said they are "not declaring war on people who queue", and reported "a 20% increase in like-for-like sales compared with the previous week", which Glenn put down to quicker service. Sources: [Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/hackney-pub-begs-customers-stop-061500075.html), [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/hackney-bar-sees-business-boom-after-urging-punters-to-stop-single-file-queuing). The project's notes say 25% (East London Times); the indexed coverage says 20%. Treat the figure as the brewery's own and keep it off the identity.
- Staff were "under strict instruction to tell people to move forward, but customers are reluctant to do so, as queue jumping is considered social suicide in the UK". Source: [BritBrief](https://britbrief.co.uk/business/retail/london-brewery-begs-customers-to-stop-bar-queuing.html).
- The exact capitalised line "A BAR IS NOT A POST OFFICE" was not found in any indexed page. "Not a post office" is confirmed. No sign, sticker or graphic is described. The visual, again, is a text post.
- The reaction shows the risk this identity has to manage: one commenter replied "Silly us, we shouldn't be so polite and respectful" ([Yahoo](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/hackney-pub-begs-customers-stop-061500075.html)). People hear "don't queue" as "be rude".

A former bar worker on r/AskABrit: a visible queue "is really appreciated because staff can get lost in an order and forget who was next" ([r/AskABrit, Oct 2023](https://reddit.fabiomanganiello.com/r/AskABrit/comments/175c0jz/kinda_curious_is_there_still_a_certain_etiquette/k4fu1oa)).

**What the landscape says about words.** The right behaviour is described, across sources, as: stand at the bar, spread out, along the bar, use the whole bar, come forward, find a gap, catch the eye, elbows on the bar, the bar staff know who's next. The wrong behaviour is always "single file" or "a line". Nobody but this project calls the right thing "The Cluster". Nobody uses "don't queue" as the positive instruction except the Glasgow sign, and that one swears.

## 2. Visual language precedents for "not this, this" marks

### Prohibition versus instruction

- **The red circle and diagonal** is the ISO 3864 / ISO 7010 "P" (prohibition) family: red ring, white field, black pictogram, diagonal from upper left to lower right, red covering at least 35% of the sign. Its origin is traced to a 1931 meeting on the Geneva Convention on road signs. Sources: [Wikipedia, ISO 7010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_7010), [Wikipedia, No symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_symbol), [Unicode U+20E0 notes](https://codepoints.net/U+20E0). Informal names: "no symbol", "do not sign", "universal no" ([Wikipedia, DO NOT](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/DO_NOT)). It is so strong that it overrides whatever sits inside it: a figure in a red ring with a slash reads as "no people", not "not like this". That is the exact misreading the feedback describes. One columnist notes the line is a backslash, and that the Ghostbusters logo is the one famous case of the opposite slant ([Creators](https://www.creators.com/read/rob-kyff-word-guy/07/16/going-around-in-circles)). The symbol's pop-culture life is a ban joke, which is wrong for a campaign that likes queues.
- **The blue disc** is the "M" (mandatory) family: white pictogram on a blue circle, blue at least 50% of the sign, "must do" rather than "must not". A blue circle with a diagonal does not exist as "mandatory". Sources: [HSE, mandatory signs](https://hse.gov.uk/workplacetransport/safetysigns/mandatory.htm), [Wikipedia, ISO 7010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_7010). This is the readable "do this" convention, and it never carries a slash.
- **The ring without a slash** already belongs to this brand (the Cluster roundel and the stamp). A ring on its own is neutral: badge, seal, roundel. Add a diagonal and it becomes a ban. Keep the ring, never add the bar.

### UK public information style

- The COI (1946 to 2011) ran short, direct fillers: Charley Says, the Green Cross Code Man, Tufty, Dark and Lonely Water. "Short, direct messages warned viewers off myriad dangers." The archive is at the BFI. Sources: [BFI Replay collection](https://replay.bfi.org.uk/collection/148), [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/charley-says-watch-these-10-classic-coi-adverts), [Marketing Week](https://marketingweek.com/coi-a-history-in-ads). The site's hazard test already borrows this voice ("Public Information Cartoon"). The mark can borrow its graphic manners too: Tom Eckersley's RoSPA safety posters "use block colours and cut out shapes" ([Archives Hub, Eckersley collection](https://archiveshub.ac.uk/search/archives/1c3e8b9a-e260-3602-9cad-bd7da95966c4)).
- **The Tidyman** (Keep Britain Tidy, on bins and packaging from 1969) is the best British precedent for a one-colour "do this" figure: one stick figure dropping litter into a bin. It shows the right act and never the wrong one, and it is understood at any size. Sources: [Keep Britain Tidy, Our history](https://www.keepbritaintidy.org/our-history), [Mental Floss on its unknown designer](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/92705/crowdsourced-search-iconic-symbols-creator).
- **TfL's "Baby on Board" badge** (launched 2006 after the "Mind the Bump" study found pregnant passengers waited about five stops for a seat) is a badge that asks for a behaviour from other people, politely, with no prohibition in it. Sources: [TfL newsroom](https://tfl-newsroom.prgloo.com/news/tfl-marks-20-years-of-baby-on-board-badges-with-new-babies-on-board-design), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_on_board). It is a precedent for the beer mat and a pin badge: an object that says "this is how it works here".

### Pictogram conventions for queues

- There is no registered public information symbol for "queue". ISO 7001:2023's list (museum, toilets, check-in, lockers, telephones) has none; the nearest is check-in: "Human figure at representation of desk or counter greeting another human figure". Sources: [Wikipedia, ISO 7001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_7001), [ANSI blog on ISO 7001:2023](https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/iso-7001-2023-registered-public-information-symbols/). So the field is open, and whatever this brand draws will not clash with a standard.
- The stock convention for "queue" is a row of identical standing figures, evenly spaced, seen straight on, sometimes with a counter at one end ([Adobe Stock queue icons](https://stock.adobe.com/images/queue-icon-sign-with-people-standing-and-waiting-in-line-in-front-of-counter-vector-illustration-transparent-background-ordered-set-for-service-customer-line-waiting-concept/1741589230)). This matters: **a row of same-size figures reads as "a line"**, whatever the intent. That is a problem for any mark that shows three people side by side, including the current roundel, because side by side along a bar and single file are the same silhouette from the front. The difference between the two behaviours is only visible from above, or from behind with the bar in view.
- The Isotype rule explains why: in Neurath and Arntz's system, "a greater quantity should not be represented by an enlarged pictogram but instead by a greater number of pictograms repeated at the same size". Repetition means quantity, and a repeated figure means "lots of people", not "a position". Sources: [Isotype Revisited](https://isotyperevisited.org/2012/08/introduction.php), [Wikipedia, Isotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_(picture_language)).
- The figure itself: the AIGA/DOT "Helvetica man" (Cook and Shanosky, 1974, 34 symbols, 50 by 1979, copyright-free) is the default silhouette for a person in signage. Sources: [AIGA Symbol Signs](https://aiga.org/resources/symbol-signs), [Wikipedia, DOT pictograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DOT_pictograms). Using it says "official notice", which fits the hazard-test voice.
- Baggage on the word "queue" in interface icons: in Material and most icon sets a "queue" icon is a playlist (three lines and a plus, or a music note). Source: [Material "queue play next"](https://www.shadcn.io/icon/material-symbols-queue-play-next-sharp). Nobody will confuse the mark with it, but a favicon that is three horizontal lines will read as a menu or a playlist, not a bar.

### Contrast in one mark, one colour

- The searches found no well-known mark that shows a true before and after inside one shape. The nearest device is figure-ground: the Rubin vase, the FedEx arrow, the NBC peacock, where the white shape changes what the black shape means. Sources: [ebaqdesign](https://www.ebaqdesign.com/blog/negative-space-logo-design), [Fabrik](https://fabrikbrands.com/famous-logos-that-use-negative-space/). The usable lesson: in one colour, the counter (the white) can carry the second reading. A bar drawn as a white gap through a black ring, with the figures as holes in the ring, is one shape with two readings.
- The safer precedent is a **pair**: a right and a wrong, both in the same style, tick and cross, "do and don't". The pair is universal in guideline signage ([Adobe Stock do and don't sets](https://stock.adobe.com/images/green-tick-red-cross-art-design-with-text-do-and-don-t-right-or-wrong-true-or-false/347398647)), and it does not rely on colour if the glyphs are distinct, which matters for colour-blind viewers ([Ben Terrett, button ideas](https://benterrett.com/blog/archive/noisydecentgraphics.typepad.com/design/2008/02/button-ideas.html)). The pair belongs on a mat or a poster, not in a favicon.

### What survives at favicon size

- Guidance agrees: at 16px, "thin details disappear", text is unreadable beyond one or two letters, and flat, bold shapes win. Google now prefers 48px or larger. Sources: [Solid Pixels](https://academy.solidpixels.com/en/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-favicon), [Ramotion](https://www.ramotion.com/blog/what-is-favicon/), [Font Awesome icon design](https://fontawesome.com/docs/web/add-icons/upload-icons/icon-design), [Productboard 16px icon guidelines](https://design.productboard.com/latest/foundations/design/iconography/guidelines-hIPSBEVC-hIPSBEVC).
- Readable at 16px: a ring, a disc, a single thick bar, one or two blobs, a diagonal. Not readable: three separate figures with heads and shoulders, a gap between figures, any word, the difference between "along" and "in a row".
- Carries baggage at 16px: a diagonal through a ring (a ban), three horizontal lines (a menu), a ring with a dot (a record button, a target).

Phrases with the most potential as a name or line, judged on whether they say "there is a queue, it just isn't a line":

1. **"The bar is the queue."** Says the whole argument in five words. Already said in the wild.
2. **"Who's next?"** The barperson's line. Implies a queue exists and someone keeps it. Also a question the mark could ask.
3. **"The invisible queue."** Kate Fox's term, twenty years old, with authority. Hard to draw, which is either the problem or the brief.
4. **"Stand at the bar."** The Sheffield sign. Positive, plain, no "don't".
5. **"You're not queue jumping."** The permission slip. Good for a mat back or a hazard-test line.
6. **"Catch the eye."** The YouGov wording. Gentle, and it names the mechanism.

## 5. Implications for the mark and name (one page)

**The problem in one line.** Every word in the current name is about stopping queueing, and the roundel shows three people in a row, which is the stock icon for a queue. The mark and the name agree with each other, and both say the wrong thing.

**What the identity must say.** Three things, in this order: (1) there is a queue; (2) it runs along the bar, not away from it; (3) the barperson keeps it. The counter-argument the campaign faces is "fairness" (YouGov's 40%, Wetherspoon's "fair service"). An identity that shows order, not a scrum, wins that argument before the copy starts.

**What to show.**
- The bar, as the main shape, with people along it. The bar is the only thing that tells "along" from "in a line". From the front, both look the same. So the view is from above (a bar as a thick line, figures as dots along it) or from behind the bar (the barperson's view, which is also the view the hazard test already uses). The current roundel is from behind the customers, so the bar is behind them, and the figures become the row.
- Order. Numbers, or a sequence, or one figure being pointed at. "Who's next?" drawn is a barperson's arm or an eyeline to one of the dots. That is the invisible queue made visible.
- A positive form. A ring, a disc or a badge, with the behaviour inside it. Never a slash.

**What to avoid.**
- Any diagonal through a ring. It means "banned" to everyone and it will read as "no people" or "no queueing" at 16px.
- Three identical figures in a row with nothing else. Isotype logic makes that "a crowd" or "a line".
- "Don't" and "stop" as the first word of the name. The sign that worked in Sheffield says "Please stand at the bar". Hackney's line that got shared says "use the whole bar". The petition's line is "queue around the bar". The positive form is the convention in the wild.
- Words inside the mark. Keep the stamp for that job.
- Calling the right behaviour "The Cluster" in the identity. It is a fine in-joke for the site, but as a name it tells a stranger nothing about queues.

**Three design cues to steal.**
1. **The Tidyman.** One figure, one colour, doing the right thing, no cross and no slash, legible on a crisp packet. Draw the right behaviour and let the wrong one be implied. ([Keep Britain Tidy](https://www.keepbritaintidy.org/our-history))
2. **The blue mandatory disc.** A filled circle with the pictogram knocked out in the counter colour means "do this" without a word. The reversed roundel (cream figures on ink) is already halfway there. Keep the fill, lose nothing. ([HSE](https://hse.gov.uk/workplacetransport/safetysigns/mandatory.htm))
3. **Figure-ground, FedEx style.** In one colour, let the white carry the second meaning. The bar as a white gap through a black disc, the people as white dots along it, numbered 1, 2, 3 if the size allows. Big, it reads "an orderly queue along a bar". Small, it reads "a bar with three dots", which is still right. ([ebaqdesign](https://www.ebaqdesign.com/blog/negative-space-logo-design))

**On the name.** If it changes, "The Bar Is The Queue" is the only candidate that states the rule rather than the ban, it is unclaimed as far as search can tell, and it already comes out of people's mouths. "Who's Next?" is the better tagline than name, because it is the barperson's line and the mark can ask it. If the name stays, the fix is in the mark and the strapline: pair "Don't Queue At The Bar" with "the bar is the queue" wherever the lockup appears, so the first line is the joke and the second is the rule.

**Open checks before any of this is final.** Handles on X, Instagram and TikTok for the chosen name. The UK IPO register and Companies House. The YouGov regional and age split quoted in PRODUCT.md. The exact Hackney Church post wording and date. Kate Fox's exact sentences if "the invisible queue" goes on anything printed.
